02 — Origin

Kiwi is a Caspian crop, not an Iranian one.

Iran grows kiwi in one place that matters: the humid subtropical strip between the Alborz range and the Caspian Sea. Mazandaran and Gilan have the rainfall, the mild winters and the long autumn that Hayward needs.

That geography is the commercial argument. Hayward harvested on the Caspian belt comes in late — October into December — which places Iranian fruit in the window after the Southern Hemisphere season closes and before European stock is fully in distribution.

It also explains why this document spends most of its length on maturity and cold chain. A late-harvested kiwi with good dry matter stores well; the same fruit picked early and handled warm does not. The origin gives us the potential. Post-harvest discipline is what delivers it.

What the Caspian belt gives Hayward
01

Humidity

Hayward does not tolerate dry heat. The Caspian strip holds the humidity the vine needs through the growing season.

02

A long autumn

Dry matter accumulates late. A mild autumn lets fruit stay on the vine long enough to reach it.

03

A late window

Harvest from October places Iranian fruit between the Southern Hemisphere season closing and European stock reaching distribution.

Growing region

Mazandaran and Gilan — the Caspian belt, between the Alborz range and the sea.

Harvest window

October to December, with storage extending supply into the following spring.

Extra · calibres 30–42

At or above the 90 g minimum. The premium size band, and the one most European programmes are written around.

Class I · calibres 42–48

At or above 70 g. Calibre 42 straddles the boundary and is declared according to the lot's actual average weight.

Class II · calibres 54–60

Between 65 g and 72 g — below the Class I floor. Sound fruit, correctly declared. Not marketed as Class I.

Why calibre 42 is split

Its 88–94 g band crosses the 90 g Extra threshold. A lot averaging 91 g is Extra; the same calibre averaging 89 g is Class I. The declaration follows the lot's measured average weight, not the calibre number.

14 — Phytosanitary

A phytosanitary certificate, issued for one destination.

Every shipment shall comply with the phytosanitary requirements applicable to entry into the European Union and be accompanied by the required certificate.

Legal basis

Fresh Actinidia fruit was added, from 1 September 2019, to the list of products requiring a mandatory phytosanitary certificate for entry into the EU — amendment (EU) 2019/523 to Council Directive 2000/29/EC, now consolidated under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/2072. Kiwi is not among the five exempt products (pineapple, banana, coconut, durian, date).

Iranian export-side process
01

Declaration at export customs

The consignment is declared at the customs office of export prior to loading.

02

PPOI inspection

Inspection by the Plant Protection Organization of Iran; where necessary the lot is treated or disinfected.

03

Certificate issued

Issued according to the destination country's stated quarantine conditions.

Destination-specific — read this before a new market

The certificate is issued for a specific destination country and commodity. A certificate obtained for one EU member state cannot be substituted for another. Any member-state-specific Additional Declaration required for Actinidia spp. should be confirmed with PPOI and cross-checked against the current consolidated text of Regulation (EU) 2019/2072 and TRACES NT before the first shipment to a new destination.

On bacterial canker (Psa)

Pseudomonas syringae pv. actinidiae is the main quarantine-relevant disease of Actinidia worldwide. It spreads via nursery stock — plants for planting — and pollen. There is no recognised pathway of introduction via fresh kiwifruit, so it is not expected to generate additional certification burden for fruit-only shipments.

This specification does not replace confirmation of the current EU pest list by PPOI or a customs consultant prior to each shipment. Regulatory text is cited as at the revision date of this document and should be verified against the consolidated version in force.
